Module-by-Module Retention Moves
One mnemonic and one activity for every phase of the NHA CCMA curriculum — organized the way you actually teach it. The hardest modules get extra attention.
📋 NHA CCMA Aligned 🏫 High School Seniors ✅ Any Classroom Structure ⭐ Hard Module Callouts
This section gives you a mnemonic and a classroom activity for every phase of the NHA CCMA curriculum. Use the mnemonic as a quick recall anchor — something students can reach for the moment they go blank on an exam question. Use the activity to build the retrieval muscle before test day arrives. Modules marked ★ THIS ONE'S HARD include an extra note on why students typically struggle and what to watch for.
Phase 1 · Weeks 1–5
Foundation
The retention challenge in Phase 1 is abstract content. Roles, ethics, law, and terminology don't feel clinical yet — students aren't sure why it matters. Anchor everything to a "what happens if you get this wrong on the job" moment and the engagement flips immediately.
Roles & Responsibilities Communication Ethics & Law A&P + Terminology Infection Control

NHA Module 1 + 12
Roles & Responsibilities
🧠 Mnemonic
PACE
Patient care first
Accountability to your scope
Communication with the team
Ethics in every interaction
🎯 Activity
Scope or Nope?
Read 10 clinical scenarios aloud. Students write "Scope" or "Nope" on a whiteboard or index card — is this within the MA's scope of practice or not? Debrief each answer with a one-sentence rationale. Fast, competitive, zero prep.
⏱ 10–12 min
🗣️
NHA Module 12
Communication & Customer Service
🧠 Mnemonic
CLEAR
Concise message
Listen actively
Empathy first
Ask to confirm understanding
Respect all barriers
🎯 Activity
Telephone Triage Role-Play
Pairs take turns as the MA and a "patient" calling with a concern. One student has a script with hidden emotional cues (anxious, confused, angry). The "MA" must respond professionally. Class votes on whether the communication was effective and names one thing they'd change.
⏱ 12–15 min
⚖️
NHA Module 13
Medical Ethics, Law & HIPAA
🧠 Mnemonic
HIPAA BITES
Breach must be reported
Identifiers must be protected (18 of them)
Treat minimum necessary info only
Employees need training
Sign the authorization before releasing records
🎯 Activity
HIPAA Violation or Not?
Present 8 realistic scenarios (texting a patient's DOB, faxing to the wrong number, discussing a patient in the elevator). Students hold up a red or green card. For every "violation," they must name which HIPAA rule was broken. Keeps the energy up and the content concrete.
⏱ 10 min
🧬
NHA Module 2
A&P Overview + Medical Terminology
🧠 Mnemonic
BUILD IT
Build terms from parts
Use prefix + root + suffix
Identify the root first
Look at the suffix for condition/procedure
Don't memorize — decode
IT gets easier with practice
🎯 Activity
Term Deconstruction Race
Write 5 medical terms on the board. Teams race to break each one into prefix, root, and suffix and write a plain-English definition. First team with all 5 correct wins. The key rule: no dictionaries, no phones — only word part logic. Builds the skill the NHA actually tests.
⏱ 10–12 min
🧤
NHA Module 6
Infection Control & Safety
🧠 Mnemonic
SAFE CHAIN
Susceptible host
Agent (pathogen)
Force of entry (portal of entry)
Exit portal
Chain must be broken at any link
Hand hygiene breaks the most
Asceptic technique matters
IN = intervention stops spread
🎯 Activity
Break the Chain Card Sort
Give each student a card with one link in the chain of infection. Students physically stand in a chain around the room. Then read an intervention aloud ("MA performs hand hygiene"). The student whose link is broken sits down. Repeat with different interventions. Physical movement = stronger memory encoding.
⏱ 12–15 min
Phase 2 · Weeks 6–13
Clinical Skills
The retention challenge in Phase 2 is volume and overlap. Students are learning hands-on procedures while simultaneously memorizing normal values, protocols, and sequences. The mnemonics here are designed to lock in the procedural order — because sequence errors are the #1 reason students lose points on clinical check-offs and exam questions.
Vital Signs Patient Intake General Patient Care ★ Phlebotomy Lab / POC Testing ★ EKG Pharmacology
💓
NHA Module 3
Vital Signs & Patient Assessment
🧠 Mnemonic
TPRRBO
Temperature
Pulse
Respirations
Resting blood pressure
Blood oxygen (O₂ sat)
Outcome: document all six
🎯 Activity
Normal or Not? Rapid Fire
Call out a vital sign value — "pulse 110," "temp 96.2°F," "BP 118/76." Students give a thumbs up (normal) or thumbs down (abnormal) and state the normal range for that vital. No notes. Fast pace. Repeat the ones they miss. Run this as a 2-minute warm-up every day until normal ranges are automatic.
⏱ 5–8 min
🩺
NHA Modules 4 + 5
General Patient Care & Sterile Technique
🧠 Mnemonic
5 RIGHTS + 3 CHECKS
5 Rights: Right patient · Right drug · Right dose · Right route · Right time

3 Checks: When you pull it · When you prepare it · Before you give it
🎯 Activity
Medication Error Scenario Sort
Give students 6 scenario cards describing medication administration situations — some correct, some with a hidden error (wrong dose, no second identifier check, skipped route confirmation). Students identify the error and name which "Right" or "Check" was violated. Discuss as a group. Real errors, real consequences — makes it memorable.
⏱ 12 min
🩸
NHA Module 8
Phlebotomy & Specimen Collection
★ This One's Hard
⚠️ Why Students Struggle Here
Phlebotomy trips students up because it has two separate memorization challenges happening at once: the order of draw (which tube color comes first) and venipuncture sequence (the procedural steps). Students often mix up which list is which, or they memorize the order of draw and blank on the rationale behind it. The mnemonic below separates these two problems.
🧠 Mnemonic
STOP LIGHT GRAY
Order of Draw by tube color:

Sterile (Yellow — Blood cultures)
Top is Light Blue (coag)
Orange/Gold (SST — serum)
Purple/Lavender (EDTA — CBC)
Light Green (lithium heparin)
Is for Green (sodium heparin)
Gray (glucose/fluoride — last)
HT = Remember: Hemolysis ruins the Tube if order is wrong
🎯 Activity
Tube Color Lineup
Print or gather colored cards representing each tube type. Shuffle them. Students race to physically arrange the cards in correct order of draw on their desk — fastest correct lineup wins. Round 2: remove one card and students identify the missing tube and explain why its position matters. Hands-on, visual, and competitive.
⏱ 10–12 min
🔬
NHA Module 7
Lab / Point of Care Testing
🧠 Mnemonic
CLIA COUNTS
Collect specimen correctly
Label before you leave the patient
Inspect for quality issues
Analyze with calibrated equipment
Control tests run first (QC)
Outliers get reported to provider
Understand critical values
Never result without documentation
Track turnaround time
Safely dispose all sharps/biohazards
🎯 Activity
Critical Value or Routine?
Present 8 lab result scenarios with values. Students decide: is this a critical value that requires immediate provider notification, or a routine result? For each critical value, they must state what they would do next and in what timeframe. Builds clinical judgment, not just memorization.
⏱ 10 min
💗
NHA Module 9
EKG & Cardiovascular Testing
★ This One's Hard
⚠️ Why Students Struggle Here
EKG is hard for two distinct reasons. First, lead placement requires spatial memory — students can recite the lead names but blank on where they actually go on the body. Second, artifact identification looks similar to abnormal rhythms to an untrained eye, and the NHA exam tests both. Students who only memorize lead names without practicing placement almost always miss artifact questions.
🧠 Mnemonic
WHITE SNOW OVER GREEN GRASS
Limb lead electrode colors (RA · LA · RL · LL):

White = Right Arm (RA)
Snow (Black) = Left Arm (LA) — "snow clouds on the left"
Over (Green) = Right Leg (RL)
Green = Left Leg (LL)

Chest leads V1–V6 go right to left across the precordium — think of a zipper closing across the chest.
🎯 Activity
Lead Placement Labeling Map
Give students a blank outline of a human torso (front view). No labels, no reference sheet. They must draw and label all 10 electrode positions from memory — 4 limb leads and 6 precordial leads. Swap with a partner to peer-check using the answer key. Round 2: identify which lead was misplaced in a pre-drawn "mistake map." Visual + spatial = sticks much better than flashcards.
⏱ 12–15 min
💊
NHA Module 1 (Pharm)
Pharmacology & Medication Administration
🧠 Mnemonic
DO NOT GUESS
Drug classifications by suffix:

-olol = Beta blocker (BP/heart rate)
-pril = ACE inhibitor (BP)
-statin = Cholesterol
-zide = Diuretic
-mycin / -cillin = Antibiotic

DO NOT GUESS = when in doubt, look it up. That's the real clinical standard.
🎯 Activity
Drug Suffix Sort
Write 10–12 medication names on the board — real, common drugs. Students group them by classification using only the suffix as a clue. No drug list allowed. After sorting, each group presents one drug and explains what it treats and which of the 5 rights would be most critical for that drug class. Bridges pharmacology to patient safety.
⏱ 12 min
Phase 3 · Weeks 14–18
Administrative & Clinic Simulation
The retention challenge in Phase 3 is that admin content feels disconnected from the clinical work students find exciting. Frame every administrative concept as patient protection or clinic survival — if this step goes wrong, the patient doesn't get care and the clinic doesn't get paid. That reframe changes everything.
Admin Assisting ★ Billing, Coding & Insurance Care Coordination
🖥️
NHA Module 11
Administrative Assisting & Front Desk
🧠 Mnemonic
SCHED-ID-DOC
The front desk workflow in order:

SCHEDule — appointment booked, insurance pre-verified
ID — two patient identifiers confirmed at check-in
DOCument — demographics, consent, copay collected, status updated
🎯 Activity
Front Desk Simulation Sprint
One student plays the MA at the front desk. Another plays a patient checking in. Give the "patient" a scenario card with hidden variables (insurance card is expired, patient gives wrong date of birth, copay is declined). The "MA" must navigate the situation professionally using only the workflow steps. Rotate pairs every 3 minutes. Best for end-of-unit review.
⏱ 15 min
💰
NHA Module 11
Billing, Coding & Insurance
★ This One's Hard
⚠️ Why Students Struggle Here
Billing and coding is the most abstract Phase 3 content — students can't see it happening the way they can see a blood draw. The three code types (ICD-10, CPT, HCPCS) look similar on paper but serve completely different purposes, and students consistently mix up which answers the "why" vs. the "what." Add insurance terminology on top (ABN, EOB, prior auth, superbill) and it becomes overwhelming fast. The key is to give each code type one concrete anchor image it never loses.
🧠 Mnemonic
WHY · WHAT · STUFF
The only three things you need to remember:

ICD-10-CM = WHY (diagnosis — why the patient came in)
CPT = WHAT (procedure — what was done to them)
HCPCS = STUFF (equipment, supplies, meds not in CPT)

Every billing question on the NHA exam fits into WHY · WHAT · STUFF.
🎯 Activity
Code Type Scenario Sort
Write 9 patient visit items on index cards — 3 diagnoses, 3 procedures, 3 supplies/equipment. Shuffle and distribute. Students sort into three piles: ICD-10, CPT, or HCPCS. Round 2: add 3 insurance term cards (ABN, superbill, prior auth) and students must explain in one sentence when each form is used. Cheap, fast, and directly mirrors how the NHA frames these questions.
⏱ 12–15 min
🤝
NHA Module 10
Patient Care Coordination & Education
🧠 Mnemonic
REFER FIRST
Care coordination workflow:

Receive provider order for referral
Eligibility check (does insurance cover this specialist?)
Form the prior authorization request if needed
Educate the patient on next steps
Record everything in the chart
Follow up to confirm appointment was made
Inform provider if patient declines
Review transition of care documentation
Send records to receiving provider
Track for completion
🎯 Activity
Referral Relay
Give teams a patient scenario requiring a specialist referral. Each team member owns one step of the coordination process — eligibility check, prior auth, patient education, record transfer. Teams race to complete the full referral workflow in the correct order and present it to the class in under 2 minutes. Tests sequence knowledge, teamwork, and communication — all tested on the NHA exam.
⏱ 15 min

One module at a time. One mnemonic at a time.

You don't have to use all of these at once. Pick the module your students are in right now and start there. Add the activity to this week's plan and teach the mnemonic before you teach the concept. Small shifts, consistent repetition, compounding results.
